# Convenience Anchor Versus Macro Overlay Follow-Up

## Purpose

This compares two first-Varrock-follow-up shapes after the opening union ledger
was traced through twenty-nine protected action groups. It is not a route,
player-facing guide, or pruning decision. It identifies what must be proved
before the project can turn the opening into concise instructions.

The comparison uses the project's default philosophy:

- prioritize durable convenience, timely access, useful fixed XP, and passive
  clocks;
- keep hidden item, skill, quest, GP, and inventory blockers visible;
- do not turn long macroefficiency overlays into default chores unless they
  produce timely value;
- preserve every proven-guide action until the omission gates are satisfied.

## Candidate A: Convenience-anchor follow-up

### Shape

This candidate keeps the first follow-up focused on high-value durable outputs:

- complete Daddy's Home when its material relay is ready;
- complete or advance Gertrude's Cat only if the food/payment/loadout is clean;
- complete Natural History Quiz while already in Varrock;
- complete Children of the Sun while already in Varrock;
- keep required low-friction handoffs that prevent real future blockers;
- defer macro-overlays that mainly save later dialogue or collect long-horizon
  incidental items.

### Inventory and bank pressure

This candidate still needs a capacity-aware relay because Daddy's Home and
Gertrude's Cat both want bulky items. The minimum proof should include:

- bank start and end point;
- carried coins;
- saw, hammer, nails, cloth, regular planks, waxwood logs/planks;
- milk, sardine, doogle leaves, and any Gertrude-specific item state;
- whether the route carries or temporarily banks quest handoff items;
- whether Daddy's Home crate outputs fit immediately or require a bank break.

### Attention and enjoyment pressure

The convenience-anchor path is mostly dialogue, deterministic museum work,
local purchases, and material handling. Its main attention cost is inventory
management, not long repetitive training. It fits the "reasonable but safe"
guide goal if the material relay is clean.

## Candidate B: Macro-overlay follow-up

### Shape

This candidate keeps the same anchor set but layers in BRUHsailer-style route
compression:

- start Rag and Bone Man I before incidental bone sources;
- start or progress Family Crest, Romeo & Juliet, Priest in Peril, Shield of
  Arrav branch state, and other Varrock handoffs;
- buy or preserve broader supply bundles;
- continue larger log/arrowshaft conversion;
- potentially pair Varlamore/Quetzal, Chronicle, and sawmill movement more
  aggressively.

### Expected outputs

| Output | Value | Current concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rag and Bone Man I start | Enables long incidental bone collection overlay. | High cognitive load and bank clutter unless the route soon encounters many bone sources. |
| More Varrock quest starts | Saves future dialogue and can preserve multiquest state. | Many starts are low immediate fragility and may not justify default complexity. |
| Family Crest start | Opens later brother routing toward goldsmithing gauntlets. | B0aty starts much later; no immediate default deadline yet. |
| Large mobile Fletching/log stock | Converts travel into XP and arrowshaft supply. | Attention-heavy and tied to BRUHsailer Wintertodt/macro goals. |
| Broader supply hoarding | May avoid future shop trips. | Risks hiding bank clutter and item-state complexity in the guide. |

### Cash and item pressure

Macro overlay inherits all convenience-anchor costs and may also add:

- more retained logs and arrowshafts;
- Chronicle/cards or transport costs if BRUHsailer timing is copied;
- extra food or thieving supply targets;
- quest supplies bought before a near-term use;
- more carried partial-state items that crowd the Daddy's Home/Gertrude relay.

The current cash-deadline ledger does not justify speculative money-making for
these overlays. Every added purchase needs either a named deadline or a clean
reason it is free while passing through.

### Inventory and bank pressure

This path is much more likely to fail as a followable guide unless the bank
relay is explicit. It needs:

- separate "held for immediate use" versus "banked for later" labels;
- item counts for logs, shafts, feathers, planks, nails, cloth, quest foods,
  beer, bucket, pie dish, silk, and ashes;
- a policy for when to stop collecting before cognitive load exceeds value;
- a clean re-entry point if the player skips optional overlays.

### Attention and enjoyment pressure

This candidate gives strong route compression but risks becoming exactly the
kind of guide Dylan is trying to avoid: dense, brittle, and full of chores that
only pay off many hours later. It may still be valuable as an optional branch
for players who enjoy macro routing, but it should not become default without a
near-term payoff.

| Dimension | Convenience anchor | Macro overlay |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Risk of 99-style wall | Low. | Medium if Wintertodt/log/Fletching assumptions leak into default. |

## Provisional route-shaping conclusion

The convenience-anchor follow-up is the better default candidate to develop
first. It aligns with the project's philosophy by producing durable utility and
fixed useful XP without forcing a long efficiency wall.

The macro-overlay follow-up remains protected evidence and may become an
optional branch, but it should not control the default route until it passes
three tests:

1. It has multiple near-term consumers, not only abstract future efficiency.
2. Its added inventory/cash/attention load is shown in a capacity-aware relay.
3. Deferring it demonstrably causes more pain than the added complexity creates.

No action is omitted by this conclusion. The next step is to consult the guide
specialist on the narrow proposed branch boundary before marking any action as
`optional_branch`, `move`, `replace`, or `omit`.
