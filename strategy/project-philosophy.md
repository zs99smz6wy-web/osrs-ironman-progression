# Project Philosophy

This document is a strategy-layer contract. It records how the project should
make judgements; it is not a source of factual OSRS requirements, drop rates,
or route steps.

## Target account

The guide is for a newly created Ironman beginning from a source-backed,
post-Tutorial Island baseline. The mature account `On That Cab` is reference
and import-testing data only. Its levels, unlocks, inventory, bank, drops, and
collection log must never seed, rank, or otherwise bias the target route.

The final route has not yet been generated. Strategy judgements must remain
separate from sourced facts and account-state observations so that either can
be corrected without silently changing the other.

## What efficiency means

Efficiency means reaching useful milestones at the best practical time while
enjoying the breadth of the game. It does not mean pursuing 99s for their own
sake, or choosing a technically faster line that removes worthwhile content.

The project should:

- Prefer permanent convenience, transportation, utility storage and reward
  items, passive systems, and collection-log rewards that provide real account
  utility.
- Use quest XP deliberately to skip inefficient skill thresholds and avoid
  wasting later training opportunities. Quest XP is a timing tool, not merely a
  completion checklist.
- Recommend quests, minigames, diaries, skilling, exploration, and PvM when
  their access, preparation, risk, and rewards are timely for the account.
- Preserve content diversity and avoid optimizing away activities whose rewards
  or experience are useful at the current stage.

Examples of timing judgements include pursuing Void after combat level 100 and
Elite Void when its requirements and role are ready; prioritizing account-wide
utility such as the herb sack, rune pouch, gem bag, and seed box; and delaying
Mastering Mixology until the account has suitable Herblore level and herb stock
to make the activity worthwhile.

PvM is part of normal progression when the account is ready and the rewards are
useful at that point. Relevant examples include Royal Titans, Perilous Moons,
Barrows, and Corrupted Gauntlet. Readiness includes more than a nominal level:
gear, supplies, mechanics, access, risk tolerance, and a sensible reason to do
the content all matter.

## Two progression lanes

The guide should maintain both an active lane and an AFK/passive lane.

- The active lane covers quests, unlocks, travel, minigames, skilling, and PvM
  that require deliberate attention.
- The AFK/passive lane covers suitable low-attention training, resource
  gathering, passive checks, and systems that progress while attention is
  limited.

Every lane recommendation should state its stop condition and its re-entry
condition. A method should stop when its target, supplies, safety, attention
window, or marginal value changes, and it should be easy to resume later when
the relevant condition returns. AFK claims must be supported by observed
account state and method conditions rather than assumed timers, rates, or
availability.

## Reacting to the account

RNG may change the route only after the account actually observes a useful
drop, unlock, or other state change. A possible drop is never assumed as part
of the baseline route. Realised RNG can remove, defer, or re-rank a conventional
acquisition path when the replacement is genuinely useful.

The same principle applies to player readiness and preference: the framework
should expose meaningful alternatives and tradeoffs, while keeping the route
explainable rather than pretending that one score proves a universal optimum.

## Decision boundary

Factual data answers what is available, required, rewarded, or observed.
Strategic judgement answers why, when, and whether it is worth doing. The
dependency graph may connect these layers, but it must not turn an unverified
fact into a recommendation or a recommendation into a fact.

The project should build and validate the data, dependency, bypass,
account-state, AFK/passive, and scoring layers before composing the final
human-readable route.
